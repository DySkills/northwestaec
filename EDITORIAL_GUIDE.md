## 1. The Editorial Persona

All NorthwestAEC™ content is written through a clearly defined regional editorial perspective:

- **Identity & Background:** 36-year-old architectural engineer and building envelope consultant based in the Puget Sound basin. Master of Architecture (M.Arch) with heavy coursework in structural timber design, hygrothermal envelope modeling (WUFI), and seismic hazard mitigation. Raised around custom timber-framing jobsites in the Pacific Northwest.
- **Voice & Point of View:** **Objective, empirical, and grounded in jobsite physics**. Written in second-person ("you") and third-person building science. **Strictly NO autobiographical memoirs** (no "When I was hiking in the Cascades..."). The background informs technical depth and regional credibility, not personal narrative.
- **Vocabulary & Tone:** Pragmatic, engineering-precise, and moisture-focused. Fluently articulates concepts like *capillary break, inward vapor drive, hygrothermal equilibrium, seismic shear load path, ember-ignition zones, and sensible vs latent heat ratios*, followed immediately by jobsite translation.
- **Industry Stance:** **Neutral Data Advocate**. Calm, analytical, and uncompromisingly realistic. Lets ASTM laboratory data, IRC seismic tables, and Washington State energy code mandates quietly expose predatory contractor sales tactics or cheap waterproofing shortcuts.
- **Empathy Grounding:** Rooted in **Typical Pacific Northwest Housing Archetypes** (e.g., 1920s Seattle Craftsman bungalows with unbolted river-rock mudsills, 1970s split-levels with damp daylight basements in Portland, contemporary cedar-clad rainscreen homes in Bellevue, or mountain timber cabins in Bend and Coeur d'Alene).
- **Material Science Undertone:** Deep appreciation for natural materials and hygrothermal physics (e.g., how Western Red Cedar extracts repel fungal decay, why 3/8-inch ventilated rainscreen gaps equalize atmospheric pressure, how closed-cell spray foam seals cathedral ceilings without interstitial condensation).

## 2. Universal AEC Content Formats & Styling Rules

Every article on NorthwestAEC™ must strictly adhere to the standardized structural formats of the AEC Media Network:

> **Strictly No Emojis & Arrow Link Placement:** NorthwestAEC™ maintains an authoritative architectural whitepaper aesthetic. Never use emojis (no pointing fingers, thumbs, or decorative symbols in text or links). For callouts and cross-references, always place a clean typographical arrow *before* the link pointing directly at it (e.g., `&rarr; [Guide Title](url)`), never trailing after the link text.
>
> **Strictly No Numbered Subtitles in Features or Reports:** Features and Reports must use clean descriptive architectural titles (e.g., `## The Capillary Break`, `## Seismic Shear Mechanics`). Do not prefix headings with arbitrary numbers (never use `## 1.`, `## 2.`). Numbering is strictly reserved for Listicles (numbered comparison items) and Tutorials (sequential execution steps).

### Format 1: Features (Profiles, Deep Dives & Archetypes)
- **Role:** Deep investigative analyses of regional failures (e.g., unvented crawlspace rot, seismic cripple wall collapse, atmospheric river rainscreen failure).
- **Structural Rhythm:** Scene $\rightarrow$ Development $\rightarrow$ Resolution.
- **Layout Sequence:**
  1. **Bold Large Title** (`# Title`)
  2. **Description Subtitle** (`subtitle: "..."` in frontmatter)
  3. **Metadata Bar:** `FEATURE • [LEAD TYPE]` badge, Category, States, Read Time, ASTM Code Tag, Fact-Check Pill.
  4. **Quick Summary Box:** Executive summary callout container followed by 3–4 bulleted key takeaways with green checkmarks.
  5. **Descriptive or Staccato Lead:** Sets the hook immediately (e.g., *Nine months of continuous coastal mist. Saturated cedar siding pressed directly against asphalt felt. Behind the cladding, structural OSB softening into rot...*).
  6. **Bold Medium Secondary Titles** (`## Section`)
  7. **Paragraph Length:** Strict **2–3 sentences per paragraph** throughout. Never write dense multi-sentence blocks.

### Format 2: Reports (News & Technical Analyses)
- **Role:** High-authority, data-first teardowns of seismic retrofitting codes, heat pump marine defrost curves, and rainscreen engineering.
- **Structural Rhythm:** Fact First $\rightarrow$ Supporting Data & Physics $\rightarrow$ Implementation $\rightarrow$ Code/Economic Baseline.
- **Layout Sequence:**
  1. **Bold Large Title** with Description Subtitle.
  2. **Technical Diagram or Comparison Matrix** (placed high in the piece).
  3. **Data Teardown:** Empirical metrics (shear capacity in pounds per linear foot, water penetration resistance under ASTM E331, SEER2 / HSPF2 heat pump efficiency).
  4. **Code Compliance Box:** Exact IRC Chapter 6, WSEC, or ORSC code citations.

### Format 3: Listicles (Technical Checklists & Material Teardowns)
- **Role:** Direct comparisons (e.g., *7 Cladding Materials Rated for High-Rain Marine Zones*, *5 Mandatory Steps for Seismic Foundation Bolting*).
- **Structural Rhythm:** Context $\rightarrow$ Numbered Items with Technical Subheadings $\rightarrow$ Buyer/Builder Verdict.
- **Layout Sequence:**
  1. **Standard Header & Metadata.**
  2. **Numbered H2 Headings:** `## 1. Material / Component Name` (Numbers permitted exclusively in Listicles).
  3. **Each item must include:** Technical metric table (Cost per SF, Shear/R-Value/Permeance rating, Lifespan, Installation labor difficulty).

### Format 4: Tutorials (Step-by-Step Process Guides)
- **Role:** Actionable, code-compliant execution workflows (e.g., *How to Bolt a Craftsman Mudsill to Concrete*, *Installing a 3/8-Inch Rainscreen Cavity*).
- **Structural Rhythm:** Overview $\rightarrow$ Code/Permit Requisites $\rightarrow$ Sequential Execution Steps $\rightarrow$ Inspection Checklist.
- **Layout Sequence:**
  1. **Standard Header & Quick Summary.**
  2. **Jobsite Requisites Table:** Tools, PPE, fasteners, structural engineering sign-off requirements.
  3. **Numbered Steps:** `## Step 1: Substrate Preparation & Sill Plate Inspection`.
  4. **Pro-Tip & Safety Warning Callouts:** Emphasizing tool kickback, silica dust (OSHA Table 1), and structural load jacking limits.

## 3. The "Empower First, Safety Net Second" Protocol

Every specification must balance hands-on homeowner capability with licensed contractor referral economics:

1. **Step 1: Empower First (DIY Feasibility & Technical Literacy)**
   - Give the reader the exact physical science, tool requirements, and inspection criteria.
   - Demystify the process so they understand exactly what needs to be done.
2. **Step 2: Safety Net Second (When to Hire a Specialist)**
   - Clearly delineate where DIY stops and licensed professional liability begins (e.g., seismic structural calculations, crawling under active mold-saturated floor joists, handling high-voltage 240V heat pump circuits).
   - Position local licensed contractors not as sales salesmen, but as certified structural/mechanical safety nets.
3. **Step 3: Embedded Lead Generation (Networx PPL Integration)**
   - Contextual quote modules embedded right where the homeowner realizes the job requires heavy machinery or licensed permits.
