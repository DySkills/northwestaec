# Roof Moss Mitigation: Installing Zinc and Copper Strips Without Damaging Shingles

> **Quick Summary:** Pressure washing asphalt shingles to eliminate moss is the single fastest way to destroy a Pacific Northwest residential roof. High-velocity water jets dislodge protective mineral granules, fracture brittle fiberglass backing mats, and void manufacturer warranties while failing to kill deep moss spores. A building-science-approved eradication process utilizes gentle mechanical sweeping, biodegradable oxygen bleach treatments, and continuous roll-formed zinc or copper strips installed along the ridge caps. Rainwater washes minute metallic ions across the shingle planes, elevating surface pH and creating a natural, permanent biocidal barrier that stops spore germination for up to a decade.
>
> - **The Pressure Washing Granule Penalty:** Dislodging ceramic mineral granules exposes raw asphalt to ultraviolet degradation, accelerating roof aging by 50% and causing premature brittleness.
> - **Ionic Oxidation Biocide:** Rainwater interacting with exposed zinc or copper strips releases positively charged metallic ions ($Zn^{2+}$ or $Cu^{2+}$) that wash downslope to inhibit moss, lichen, and cyanobacteria.
> - **Biodegradable Chemical Chemistry:** Sodium percarbonate (oxygen bleach) breaks down organic cellular walls into soda ash and water, avoiding corrosive chlorine runoff that poisons salmon habitats.
> - **Fastener Penetration Sealing:** All mechanical fasteners securing metal ridge strips must be driven beneath shingle tabs or sealed with high-grade polyurethane sealant to prevent slow attic leaks.

## The Pressure Washer Delusion in Cascadia Roof Maintenance

When heavy autumn rains arrive in Western Washington and Oregon, homeowners look up to see dense green moss blankets blooming along roof valleys and eaves. Unscrupulous door-to-door roof maintenance contractors frequently convince property owners that high-pressure blasting with a commercial 3,500-PSI power washer is the only way to restore the roof's appearance. While the roof appears clean for several weeks, the underlying physical damage is irreversible.

Asphalt shingles are composite materials manufactured with an asphalt-impregnated fiberglass mat surfaced with ceramic-coated rock granules. These granules protect the petroleum asphalt core from ultraviolet degradation, provide Class A fire resistance, and lend color to the roof plane. High-pressure water jets blast these granules loose, washing buckets of mineral sediment directly into rain gutters.

Once the granule layer is eroded, the underlying black asphalt is exposed to the elements, where solar radiation rapidly oxidizes the oils and turns the shingle brittle. Microscopic cracks develop across the shingle surface, curling the edges and allowing subsequent winter winds to tear entire tabs off the roof deck. According to the Asphalt Roofing Manufacturers Association (ARMA), pressure washing automatically voids all standard manufacturer material warranties.

## Step 1: Substrate Preparation and Dry Gentle Sweeping

Wait for a dry, overcast morning when the roof surface is not dangerously slick from morning frost or active rain. Mount an OSHA-compliant reusable roof anchor into a solid structural rafter near the roof peak, attach your personal fall arrest harness, and connect your lifeline. Never walk on a damp moss-covered asphalt roof without complete fall protection.

Using a soft-bristle nylon broom or telescoping roof brush, gently sweep away loose conifer needles, pine cones, and dry moss clumps. Work strictly from the top ridge downward, brushing parallel with the shingle tabs to avoid prying them upward. Never scrub aggressively or use stiff wire rakes that scratch ceramic granules off the surface.

Collect the swept debris directly from the gutters rather than washing it down the downspouts. Heavy accumulations of pine needles and damp moss will quickly clog subterranean perimeter drains and rain garden collection basins. Once loose debris is evacuated, inspect the shingle field for cracked tabs, lifted nail heads, or broken factory sealant strips.

---

## Step 2: Applying the Oxygen Bleach Biocidal Solution

Mix one pound of powdered sodium percarbonate into two gallons of warm water inside a clean backpack sprayer until the chemical crystals completely dissolve. Sodium percarbonate is an adduct of sodium carbonate and hydrogen peroxide that produces active oxygen upon contact with water. Unlike toxic chlorine bleach, oxygen bleach does not dissolve the asphalt binder or corrode aluminum gutters.

Starting from the lowest row of shingles and moving progressively upward, spray the solution generously across all moss-infested roof planes until the shingle surface is thoroughly saturated. Allow the oxygen bleach solution to dwell on the roof for 30 to 45 minutes without drying out. The expanding micro-bubbles penetrate the cellular walls of moss rhizoids and black algae colonies (*Gloeocapsa magma*), breaking down their cellular matrix.

Rinse the roof with a standard low-pressure garden hose equipped with a gentle brass spray nozzle. You will observe the moss turning from bright green to an inert yellow-brown or white color as the cellular chlorophyll dies. Do not attempt to forcefully scrape the dead moss away immediately; winter rain and natural thermal expansion will wash the dead, desiccated biological matter down the roof over the next several weeks.

---

## Step 3: Sizing and Positioning Metallic Zinc Strips

To prevent airborne moss spores from recolonizing the clean shingles, you must install continuous metallic zinc or copper strips along the roof ridges. As rainwater washes over the exposed metal, it leaches minute amounts of elemental zinc ions ($Zn^{2+}$) or copper ions ($Cu^{2+}$) into solution. This slightly metallic rainwater washes down the roof slope, creating an environment where moss and lichens cannot chemically survive.

Measure the total linear footage of all horizontal roof ridges and hips that sit above shaded, tree-covered slopes. A single 2.5-inch wide continuous zinc strip protects approximately 14 to 16 linear feet of downhill shingle slope. For steep roofs with rafter runs exceeding 18 feet, you must plan a secondary intermediate row of zinc strips halfway down the roof pitch to maintain effective chemical ion concentrations.

Unroll the 99.9% pure zinc coil and cut it into manageable 10-foot lengths using aviation snips. Keep the material flat and avoid sharp creasing that could weaken the metal strip over time. If your home features copper architectural accents or gutters, specify solid copper rolls instead of zinc to prevent galvanic corrosion between dissimilar metals.

---

## Step 4: Installing and Fastening the Ridge Strips

Carefully slide the upper 1.5 inches of the zinc strip beneath the bottom edge of the ridge cap shingles. Approximately 1.0 to 1.5 inches of bare metallic zinc must remain exposed directly to sky and rainfall to ensure sufficient surface area for ionic oxidation. If working beneath a standard field shingle course, gently lift the overlapping tab with a wide putty knife.

Secure the strip using 1.25-inch hot-dipped galvanized ring-shank roofing nails driven every 12 inches on center along the concealed upper edge. In high-wind coastal zones, reduce fastener spacing to 8 inches on center to prevent wind flutter during coastal gales. Ensure fasteners do not penetrate through open keyways between adjacent shingle tabs.

Before driving each nail home, apply a generous dab of Class 25 polyurethane elastomeric sealant over the fastener penetration point. Slide the shingle tab back into position and press firmly to bond the shingle to the sealant. If ambient temperatures are below 50°F, apply a quarter-sized dab of plastic asphalt roof cement beneath each lifted shingle tab to re-establish the wind-uplift seal.

---

## Step 5: Ongoing Annual Maintenance Protocol

Even with active zinc strips installed, roof planes require annual inspection to ensure unhindered ion washdown. Every autumn after deciduous leaves fall, inspect the zinc strips to verify they have not become buried beneath heavy layers of wet fir needles or oak leaves. If debris blankets the metal strip, rainwater cannot make contact with the metallic surface, neutralizing its biocidal protection.

Gently sweep away accumulated needles using your soft-bristle broom from the perimeter ladder line. Check the exposed zinc strip for signs of heavy white oxidation or mechanical lifting caused by wind storms. Over five to ten years of continuous rainfall, the sacrificial zinc metal will gradually dissolve; when the metal becomes paper-thin or tears, replace the strips following this same procedure.
