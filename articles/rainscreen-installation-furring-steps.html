How to Install a Cascadia Rainscreen: Sizing Furring Strips, Bug Screens & Flashing Protocol

A practical jobsite carpentry guide to installing ventilated exterior rainscreens: Coroplast vs treated wood furring, bottom bug screen wraps, and window Z-flashing.

By Northwest AEC Building Science • Published October 2026 • ASTM E2273 / IRC Section R703.1.1 / WSEC-R Section R402.4
Quick Summary

In the Pacific Northwest, face-nailing siding directly over housewrap without a drainage gap is the leading cause of hidden sheathing rot and toxic mold. Continuous marine rain creates capillary water tension that pulls moisture through nail holes into structural OSB. Installing an engineered rainscreen cavity creates a physical capillary break that drains 99% of bulk water while functioning as a convective drying chimney.

  • Required Skill Level: Intermediate Siding / Carpentry
  • Estimated Project Duration: 1 to 2 days per 1,000 square feet of exterior wall
  • Primary Code Mandate: IRC Section R703.1.1 and Seattle DCI Director's Rule 4-2012.

Step 1: Inspecting & Sealing the Water-Resistive Barrier (WRB)

The water-resistive barrier (housewrap or self-adhered membrane) functions as the actual drainage plane of the building envelope. Once the rainscreen furring strips and siding are installed, correcting defects behind the drainage plane is impossible.

Inspect the entire wall surface for tears, un-taped seams, or over-driven staples. Seal all horizontal and vertical WRB laps using certified 4-inch acrylic flashing tape (such as Siga Wigluv or Tyvek Tape).

Ensure the WRB laps shingle-style over the foundation sill flashing at the bottom of the wall, extending at least two inches past the wooden mudsill onto the concrete foundation.

Step 2: Installing the L-Shaped Perforated Bug Screen

At the base of the wall (typically two inches above finished grade, paving, or roof intersections), install continuous perforated aluminum or stainless steel bug screen.

Align the L-shaped bug screen along a laser level line and fasten it through the WRB into the bottom plate using corrosion-resistant staples or short roofing nails:

  • The horizontal leg of the screen extends outward to the planned depth of your siding assembly.
  • The 1/8-inch stamped perforations allow bulk water to drain instantly and fresh air to enter freely while forming an impenetrable barrier against carpenter ants, wasps, and mice.

Never apply caulk or expanding foam along the bottom edge of the bug screen; sealing this joint traps drainage water inside the wall.

Step 3: Fastening Vertical Furring Strips on 16-Inch Centers

Snap vertical chalk lines directly over the centerline of every framing stud (typically 16 inches on center).

Fasten 3/8-inch to 3/4-inch furring strips vertically along these chalk lines:

  • Treated Wood Battens (1x2 or 1x3): Must be pressure-treated or rot-resistant cedar. Fasten using 2-inch hot-dip galvanized ring-shank nails driven directly into structural wall studs.
  • Ventilated Coroplast Strips (DCI Cor-A-Vent or Keene): Dual-wall extruded polypropylene battens that allow omni-directional airflow and zero rot risk. Fasten with standard 1-1/2-inch corrosion-resistant staples or nails.

Leave a 1/2-inch vertical gap between furring strip ends every 8 feet to allow cross-ventilation and prevent water bridging across joints.

Step 4: Window Head Z-Flashing & Drainage Clearance

Integrating window openings into a rainscreen requires careful management of gravity drainage above window casings.

Install rigid 26-gauge prepainted aluminum or stainless steel Z-flashing directly above the top window trim casing. The upper vertical leg of the Z-flashing must slip behind the WRB above, while the horizontal leg slopes slightly downward with a 1/4-inch hemmed drip edge extending past the siding face.

Terminate the vertical rainscreen furring strips 1/2 inch above the top of the metal Z-flashing. This intentional gap ensures that water draining down the wall drops onto the metal flashing and sheds outward, rather than pooling behind the window head casing.

Fasten siding planks (fiber cement, cedar bevel, or engineered wood) through the furring strips directly into structural wall framing using ring-shank nails sized to penetrate at least 1-1/4 inches into solid wood studs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the minimum rainscreen gap thickness in the Pacific Northwest?

Building science codes (including Seattle DCI Rule 4-2012) recommend a minimum 3/8-inch (9.5 mm) capillary drainage gap for fiber cement, cedar, and engineered wood claddings in Marine Zone 4C. High-exposure coastal zones benefit from a 3/4-inch (19 mm) cavity to maximize convective airflow and rapid drying.

Can I install siding horizontally over vertical furring strips?

Yes. Standard horizontal lap siding (such as James Hardie or cedar bevel siding) nails directly to vertical furring strips on 16-inch centers. If you are installing vertical siding (such as board-and-batten), you must install vertical furring first, followed by horizontal purlins or a cross-ventilated matrix.

Do Coroplast furring strips hold siding nails?

No. Furring strips only maintain the drainage gap spacing; they do not carry structural nail withdrawal loads. Siding nails must be sized long enough to pass completely through the siding, through the furring strip, and penetrate at least 1-1/4 inches into the structural 2x4 or 2x6 wall framing studs behind the sheathing.

What happens if I forget the bottom insect screen?

Without an insect screen, the continuous 3/8-inch to 3/4-inch open cavity becomes an ideal nesting habitat for yellowjackets, paper wasps, carpenter ants, and field mice. Installing an L-shaped perforated screen or Coroplast vent strip is mandatory before attaching the first siding course.
