# Step-by-Step Pacific Northwest Crawlspace Encapsulation & Radon Sealing Protocol

> **Quick Summary:** In the Pacific Northwest, vented dirt crawlspaces act as moisture sponges that rot floor joists and draw radioactive radon gas up into living spaces via the stack effect. Transforming a wet, vented dirt crawlspace into a clean, conditioned space requires grading the soil, laying an 8-ounce geotextile puncture cushion, sealing a 20-mil virgin polyolefin vapor retarder to foundation stem walls, and installing a dedicated commercial low-temperature dehumidifier.
>
> - **Required Skill Level:** Intermediate DIY / Waterproofing Specialist
> - **Estimated Project Duration:** 2 to 3 days for a 1,200 sq ft crawlspace
> - **Primary Standards:** International Residential Code (IRC Section R408.3) and ASTM E1745 Class A puncture resistance.

## Step 1: Debris Clearing, Grading & Sub-Membrane Radon Piping

Begin by clearing all organic debris, construction scrap lumber, fallen insulation, and sharp rocks from the crawlspace floor. Rake the soil smooth and slope the dirt floor toward a low-point collection basin or sump pit.

In EPA Radon Zone 1 and 2 areas across Washington, Oregon, and Idaho, install a perforated 4-inch Schedule 40 PVC pipe loop directly in the center of the earthen floor. Cover the perforated pipe with clean washed gravel and cap it with a solid T-fitting.

Stub a non-perforated 4-inch vertical PVC pipe through the floor to serve as an active radon vent stack, ready to connect to a continuous inline radon mitigation fan.

---

## Step 2: Installing the Geotextile Puncture Cushion

Unroll 8-ounce non-woven geotextile cushion fabric directly over the graded dirt floor, overlapping seams by at least 6 inches. 

Pacific Northwest soils frequently contain jagged glacial till, sharp basalt rocks, and tree roots. Walking on plastic vapor barriers over rocky soil creates thousands of microscopic punctures that allow soil gas and moisture vapor to escape.

The geotextile underlayment creates a permanent puncture-resistant cushion beneath the heavy vapor barrier, ensuring the membrane withstands decades of crawling traffic.

## Step 3: Rolling, Seaming & Wall-Mounting the 20-Mil Barrier

Roll out the 20-mil string-reinforced virgin polyolefin barrier across the crawlspace floor, allowing the membrane to ride up the concrete perimeter stem walls to within three inches of the wooden mudsill.

Overlap all field seams by a minimum of **12 inches**. Apply double-sided butyl tape between the overlapping sheets, roll the seam firmly with a weighted steel roller, and seal the top edge with 4-inch heavy-duty white acrylic seaming tape.

Along the exterior concrete foundation walls, scrape the concrete clean of dirt and loose efflorescence. Apply a thick, continuous bead of polyurethane construction adhesive (Sikaflex 1A), press the poly firmly into the mastic, and secure it mechanically using powder-actuated masonry fasteners every 12 inches on center.

---

## Step 4: Stem Wall Insulation & 3-Inch Termite Inspection Stripe

Fasten continuous 2-inch expanded polystyrene (EPS) or polyisocyanurate rigid foam boards (R-10 minimum) over the vapor barrier along all perimeter concrete walls.

Leave a continuous **3-inch bare concrete gap between the top of the foam and the bottom of the wooden mudsill**. This inspection stripe is mandatory under building codes, allowing pest management technicians to inspect for subterranean termite shelter tubes and carpenter ants.

Seal all foundation vent openings permanently using custom-cut rigid foam plugs fastened tightly from the interior and sealed with expanding foam.

---

## Step 5: Rim Joist Air-Sealing & Dehumidifier Setup

Air-seal and insulate the exterior rim joist and band joist cavities along the foundation perimeter using a two-component closed-cell expanding polyurethane spray foam kit. Apply a minimum of 2 inches of closed-cell foam to deliver R-13 thermal resistance and a complete airtight vapor barrier.

Install a commercial-grade, low-temperature horizontal dehumidifier (such as a Santa Fe Compact70 or Aprilaire unit) set on a level hanging bracket or vibration-dampening pad. Standard residential dehumidifiers freeze over at 55°F; commercial low-temp units operate efficiently down to 40°F.

Route the dehumidifier's gravity drain line to the sump basin or exterior daylight, and plug the unit into a dedicated 15-amp GFCI electrical circuit. Set the digital humidistat to **50% relative humidity** to ensure timber framing remains permanently dry.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use standard 6-mil black plastic from the hardware store?
No. Standard 6-mil black construction plastic is manufactured from low-grade recycled resins that break down under soil alkalinity within two to three years. It punctures easily under foot traffic and off-gasses plasticizer odors. Building codes mandate a minimum 12-mil to 20-mil multi-layer virgin polyolefin membrane conforming to ASTM E1745 Class A.

### Why do I have to leave a 3-inch gap at the top of the foundation wall?
The 3-inch bare concrete gap is a mandatory pest inspection stripe. If insulation covers the entire concrete stem wall all the way to the wooden mudsill, subterranean termites and carpenter ants can build concealed mud tunnels behind the foam directly into your floor framing without being detected.

### Does closing foundation vents cause radon gas buildup?
If you seal vents without a continuous vapor barrier, radon levels can increase. However, a properly encapsulated crawlspace with taped seams and a sub-membrane perforated PVC collection pipe stops radon gas from entering the home, providing the ideal foundation for active radon depressurization.

### How much does crawlspace encapsulation cost in the Pacific Northwest?
Professional encapsulation for an average 1,200 sq ft Pacific Northwest crawlspace typically ranges from \$5,500 to \$9,500, depending on soil clearance, drainage trenching, and commercial dehumidifier sizing. The investment typically reduces home heating costs by 15% to 25% while eliminating floor rot.
