## 1. Geographic Scope & Thermodynamic Climate Profile

- **Target States (5 States):** Washington, Oregon, Idaho, Montana, Alaska.
- **IECC / ASHRAE 90.1 Climate Zones:**
  - **Zone 4C (Marine):** Dominant west of the Cascade Crest (Seattle, Tacoma, Olympia, Portland, Eugene, Bellingham). Characterized by mild, wet winters, cool summers, and 8 to 9 months of continuous relative humidity above 80% with low solar drying potential. Heating Degree Days: 4,000 to 5,200.
  - **Zone 5B / 6B / 7 (Cold Dry & Sub-Arctic Mountain):** Inland Pacific Northwest (Spokane, Boise, Coeur d'Alene, Missoula, Bozeman, Anchorage, Fairbanks). Extreme sub-zero winter temperatures (-15°F to -40°F), heavy snow loading (60–120 PSF ground snow load), high-altitude UV, and short drying windows.
- **Core Building Physics & Structural Challenges:**
  - **Inward Bulk Water & 9-Month Saturation:** Extended low-intensity rain ("liquid sunshine") and atmospheric rivers depositing continuous water on building envelopes without solar drying cycles, leading to capillary water migration through claddings.
  - **Vented Crawlspace Moisture Inversion:** Outside 90% RH cool maritime air entering crawlspaces, condensing on 55°F subfloors and framing timbers, driving wood rot, mold proliferation, and radon migration.
  - **Cold-Roof Condensation in Marine Climates:** Warm interior moisture escaping through unsealed ceiling planes into high-humidity unconditioned attics, condensing on OSB roof sheathing that never dries out due to overcast cloud cover.
  - **Seismic Lateral Restraint (Cascadia Subduction Zone):** Strict prescriptive and engineered seismic bracing requirements (IRC Chapter 6 Wall Bracing / Seismic Design Categories D0, D1, D2, and E). Unreinforced masonry (URM) seismic retrofits, foundation sill plate bolting, and cripple wall plywood sheathing.
  - **Wildland-Urban Interface (WUI) Wildfire Hardening:** Ember-resistant eave venting (ASTM E2886), Class A standing-seam metal roofing, non-combustible rainscreens (fiber cement, metal composite), and tempered double/triple glazing.
  - **Cold-Climate Mini-Split Sizing & Defrost Dynamics:** High winter relative humidity causing rapid evaporator coil icing on air-source heat pumps between 32°F and 40°F, requiring advanced demand-defrost logic and base-pan heaters.

## 2. Dominant Regional Architectural Vernacular & Housing Archetypes

1. **Pacific Northwest Craftsman / Bungalow (1900–1935):** Seattle, Portland, Spokane. Low-pitch roofs, massive overhanging eaves, exposed decorative rafter tails, uninsulated fir framing, unreinforced river-rock or concrete masonry foundations with unbolted mudsills.
2. **Cascadia Mid-Century Split-Level & Daylight Basement (1955–1980):** Built into hillside topography. Upper level wood-framed over lower-level cast-in-place concrete or CMU daylight basement with severe subterranean moisture intrusion from uphill soil slopes.
3. **Heavy Timber & Log Post-and-Beam (1970–Present):** Mountain corridors (Bend, Whitefish, Sun Valley, Leavenworth). Large-dimension Doug Fir or Western Red Cedar timbers, requiring specialized thermal envelope insulation (SIPS or outboard mineral wool) over exposed timber decks.
4. **Modern Pacific Northwest Contemporary / Mass Timber (2000–Present):** Cross-Laminated Timber (CLT), cedar or charred Shou Sugi Ban vertical rainscreens, continuous open-joint claddings, flat or butterfly roofs with concealed interior gutters.
5. **Manufactured & Modular Rural Housing (1970–Present):** Eastern Washington, Idaho, and rural Oregon. Uninsulated crawlspace skirtings, high heating costs, freeze-prone plumbing supply runs.
